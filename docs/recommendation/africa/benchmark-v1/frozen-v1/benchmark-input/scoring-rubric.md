# Scoring Rubric — African/Nigerian Fashion Benchmark v1

**Phase:** 5C.3 Track B  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** 2026-08-19  

> **Critical rule:** These dimensions are reviewer evaluation tools. The final gold ranking
> is the reviewer's professional judgement. The rubric scores must **not** be automatically
> converted into Amodka engine scores or used to override the reviewer's ranked preference.

---

## 1. Overview

For each candidate outfit in an OR / OC / WX case, the reviewer scores seven dimensions
on a 0–5 scale. The scores help the reviewer think systematically and provide diagnostic
data for the evaluation report — they do not mechanically determine the final ranking.

**Maximum per candidate:** 35 points (7 dimensions × 5)

---

## 2. Dimension definitions

### Dimension 1 — Context suitability (0–5)

*Does this outfit match the occasion and social context described?*

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 5 | Perfectly suited. This is exactly what a competent Nigerian stylist would recommend for this context. No adjustments needed. |
| 4 | Well suited. Minor improvement possible but clearly appropriate. |
| 3 | Acceptable. Passes the context test but not the strongest choice. |
| 2 | Marginal. Would not be criticised but raises eyebrows. |
| 1 | Poorly suited. Would be noticed as inappropriate. |
| 0 | Wrong context entirely. Would be considered a clear dress-code violation. |

**Examples:**
- Ivory lace gown at a Nigerian traditional wedding ceremony → 5
- Ivory lace gown at a casual Saturday market outing → 0
- Western cocktail dress at a traditional wedding → 3 (acceptable but not ideal)
- Jeans + t-shirt at a naming ceremony → 1

---

### Dimension 2 — Cultural appropriateness (0–5)

*Does this outfit respect and reflect the cultural context of the occasion?*

This dimension is distinct from context suitability. A Western cocktail dress at a Nigerian
traditional wedding may be formally appropriate (formality level matches) but culturally
under-invested.

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 5 | Strongly culturally aligned. Demonstrates understanding of the specific occasion's cultural norms and expectations. |
| 4 | Culturally appropriate. Respects the context; a Nigerian guest would not find it out of place. |
| 3 | Culturally neutral. Neither aligned nor misaligned. Generic. |
| 2 | Culturally thin. Technically not wrong but shows no engagement with the cultural context. |
| 1 | Culturally misaligned. Something about the outfit conflicts with the occasion's cultural expectations. |
| 0 | Culturally inappropriate. Would be considered disrespectful, offensive, or seriously mismatched. |

Apply your own cultural knowledge here — including regional and religious variation in
colour symbolism, mourning conventions, and ceremony dress codes. Note the specific
tradition you are drawing on in your rationale where relevant.

---

### Dimension 3 — Visual harmony (0–5)

*Does the outfit work together as a coherent visual whole?*

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 5 | Excellent visual harmony. Colours, patterns, and proportions work together effortlessly. Would look polished in a photograph. |
| 4 | Good harmony. Minor tension but the outfit reads as considered and put-together. |
| 3 | Acceptable harmony. Passable but not memorable. |
| 2 | Weak harmony. Some pieces clash or feel mismatched. |
| 1 | Poor harmony. Noticeable visual conflict — competing patterns, clashing colours, or proportion mismatch. |
| 0 | No harmony. The pieces look randomly assembled. |

Judge colour, pattern, and proportion interplay by your own professional standards.

---

### Dimension 4 — Proportion and silhouette (0–5)

*Does the outfit create a well-proportioned, flattering silhouette?*

Note: this dimension applies within the context of the described wearer's profile. Where
the benchmark does not specify a body type, assess for a general adult woman's figure.

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 5 | Excellent proportion. The silhouette is intentional and flattering. Hemlines, volumes, and widths work together. |
| 4 | Good proportion. Generally flattering; no obvious proportion problems. |
| 3 | Acceptable proportion. Nothing actively wrong. |
| 2 | Weak proportion. One element throws off the silhouette — e.g. two very voluminous pieces, or very short top + very short bottom. |
| 1 | Poor proportion. Combination creates an awkward silhouette. |
| 0 | No coherent proportion. Multiple elements in direct conflict. |

---

### Dimension 5 — Pattern and visual-weight balance (0–5)

*Are the patterns and visual weights balanced appropriately?*

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 5 | Excellent balance. The visual hierarchy of the outfit reads exactly as you would style it. |
| 4 | Good balance. Mostly well managed; small amount of visual competition. |
| 3 | Acceptable balance. Pattern works but could be stronger. |
| 2 | Weak balance. Visual hierarchy is unclear or muddled. |
| 1 | Poor balance. Confusing visual noise. |
| 0 | Overwhelming. The outfit is visually exhausting. |

How you weigh pattern scale, pattern mixing, and coordinated same-fabric pieces is
your professional judgement — there is no prescribed rule. Please explain your
approach in your rationale, especially where patterned pieces interact.

---

### Dimension 6 — Weather suitability (0–5)

*Is this outfit appropriate for the described weather conditions?*

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 5 | Perfectly suited. Fabric weight, coverage, and accessories are all appropriate for the described temperature, rain, and humidity. |
| 4 | Well suited. One minor element could be improved (e.g. slightly warm fabric but outfit overall works). |
| 3 | Acceptable. Outfit is wearable but not optimised for the weather. |
| 2 | Marginal. Weather creates a comfort or practical problem. |
| 1 | Poor weather suitability. Outfit would cause noticeable discomfort or be practically impaired (e.g. open sandals in heavy rain). |
| 0 | Completely wrong for the weather. Would be unwearable (e.g. heavy wool coat in 38°C heat; strappy sandals in a downpour). |

**Note on Nigerian climate:**  
- 30°C+ hot/humid → heavy fabrics (lace gown, synthetic blazer, wool) → score ≤ 2
- Lagos heavy rain → open sandals, espadrilles, wicker bag → score 0–1
- 20°C Nigerian evening → light outerwear optional; most wardrobes fine without it

---

### Dimension 7 — Overall styling quality (0–5)

*Setting aside individual dimensions, how good is this outfit overall as a piece of styling?*

This is your holistic professional judgement. It may differ from the arithmetic sum of the
above dimensions — e.g. an outfit may have strong context suitability and poor visual
harmony, but your overall view is that it "works" because the cultural context forgives the
visual tension. Or an outfit may score 4 on every dimension but feel bland — score it lower.

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 5 | Excellent. A confident Nigerian fashion stylist would be proud to recommend this. |
| 4 | Good. Solid recommendation with minor room for improvement. |
| 3 | Acceptable. Would not embarrass anyone but is not a strong recommendation. |
| 2 | Weak. You would suggest a change before a client wore this. |
| 1 | Poor. A noticeable styling mistake. |
| 0 | Not acceptable. You would advise against wearing this in this context. |

---

## 3. How to use the rubric

1. **Score each candidate independently** — do not compare candidates while scoring;
   assess each one in isolation against the scenario.
2. **Record each dimension score** on the label form in `reviewer-instructions.md`.
3. **Then rank candidates** from best to worst using your professional judgement.
   The ranking should be informed by the dimension scores but is not mechanically derived
   from them.
4. **Name your gold outfit** — the single candidate you would recommend without hesitation.
5. **Write your rationale** — explain in your own words why you ranked them as you did.
   This rationale is the most valuable part of the gold label.
6. **Flag disagreement or uncertainty** — if you are genuinely unsure between two
   candidates, say so. Don't force false certainty.

---

## 4. Rubric for garment-classification cases (GU cases)

For GU cases the rubric is replaced by the classification form (see `gold-label-schema.md`).
Reviewers additionally record:

| Dimension | Scale |
|---|---|
| Classification confidence | high / medium / low |

Describe the garment in your own professional vocabulary. You are not required to fit it
into any predefined category list — if a garment needs a term that isn't on the form,
write the term you would actually use.

---

## 5. What the rubric scores are NOT

- They are **not** averaged to produce the gold ranking. The ranking is your professional judgement.
- The sum of dimension scores is used only as an aggregate quality measure in later analysis;
  it never overrides your ranked preference.
- They are **not** objective truth. Fashion involves legitimate disagreement. If a second
  reviewer scores differently, that is evidence about the case's complexity, not an error.
