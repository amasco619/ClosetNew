Phase 3.4 established that the engine has competent ranking capability, but also identified three systematic ranking weaknesses that are sufficiently evidenced to justify controlled calibration:

Focal-point competition / visual-weight overload
Weak silhouette/body-proportion weighting
Hero-pattern + solid-ground hierarchy

Phase 3.5 is the final targeted deterministic calibration phase before the end-to-end production-readiness benchmark.

2. ABSOLUTE OBJECTIVE

Improve ranking quality without redesigning the recommendation engine.

The goal is:

When multiple outfits are valid, rank the outfit with the strongest overall composition higher than an outfit that merely accumulates more individual positive signals.

This phase is specifically about correcting three evidenced ranking distortions.

5. CALIBRATION #1 — VISUAL-WEIGHT / FOCAL-POINT COMPETITION
Problem

Phase 3.4 identified a systematic problem where adding additional visually dominant elements can increase the score even when the resulting outfit is stylistically inferior.

Key examples:

CS26

Leather jacket + gold satin skirt + nude heels

Internal: 21

External: 63

versus:

Leather jacket + black jeans + white tee + black heels

Internal: 16

External: 84

CS27

Accessory-heavy five-piece outfit

Internal: 24

External: 61

versus restrained four-piece outfit

Internal: 23

External: 80

AP14

Same underlying reversal.

CS29

Leather outerwear outfit

Internal: 24

External: 63

versus cashmere + wool outfit

Internal: 22

External: 85

Phase 3.4 identified the common principle:

The engine has no concept of competing visual focal points or diminishing returns from additional statement elements.

6. DO NOT IMPLEMENT A SIMPLE "STATEMENT COUNT -X"

Do NOT blindly implement:

if statementPieces >= 2:
    score -= 3

or:

statementPieceCount * -2

That is too crude.

Some outfits legitimately contain:

statement jacket;
statement shoe;
statement bag;

and can still be excellent.

The problem is not simply the number of "interesting" pieces.

The problem is:

visual competition.

7. INVESTIGATE VISUAL WEIGHT

Determine whether the existing garment data can distinguish meaningful visual dominance through combinations of:

pattern;
gloss/lustre;
saturated colour;
high contrast;
statement texture;
silhouette prominence;
hero designation;
accessory prominence;
material richness.

Do not assume every one of these signals should count equally.

Determine which signals already exist.

8. PREFER RELATIONSHIP LOGIC

If the existing architecture supports it, favour something conceptually like:

visualWeight(item)

and:

focalCompetition(outfit)

rather than simply:

statementPieceCount(outfit)

The objective is to detect situations such as:

strong hero + strong secondary hero

or:

hero + multiple competing statement accessories

rather than penalising all visually interesting outfits.

9. PROTECT INTENTIONAL LAYERING

A good outfit may legitimately contain:

textured hero;
premium material;
restrained accessory.

Do not penalise the combination merely because multiple items contribute visual interest.

The desired behaviour is:

one dominant focal point + supporting elements

rather than:

one dominant focal point + multiple competing focal points.

10. CONTROLLED BOUNDS

Any new focal-competition adjustment must be bounded.

It must not overpower:

occasion;
formality;
weather;
personalisation;
colour compatibility;
proportion.

Document:

minimum adjustment;
maximum adjustment;
typical adjustment.

It should prefer:

12. CALIBRATION #2 — SILHOUETTE / BODY-PROPORTION WEIGHTING
Problem

Phase 3.4 produced:

0/3 Top-1 in Silhouette

with mean regret of 7 points.

Phase 3.4 identified that:

heightProportion;
bodyTypeProportion;

currently operate in approximately a ±1–2 point range.

Meanwhile:

fabric;
completeness;
palette;

can create 5–10 point swings.

Therefore legitimate body-proportion signals can be overwhelmed.

13. DO NOT BLINDLY MULTIPLY BY 3

The report suggested ×2–3.

Do not simply implement:

Instead perform controlled sensitivity analysis.

Test:

Current weighting.

Variant B

~2×.

Variant C

~2.5×.

~3×.

Use the benchmark to determine the appropriate range.

16. CALIBRATION #3 — HERO PATTERN + SOLID GROUND
Problem

Phase 3.4 identified CS05:

Floral hero + solid black midi

External: 78

was ranked below:

Solid navy + solid black

External: 66

because pattern presence triggered a penalty.

The current engine treats pattern largely as:

risk

rather than:

potentially useful focal interest when properly grounded.

17. DO NOT IMPLEMENT "PATTERN = GOOD"

Do NOT simply add:

patternedHero += 3

That would reverse the problem.

The desired relationship is:

one intentional pattern + solid supporting garments + no competing pattern = potentially positive

not:

pattern = positive.

18. DEFINE HERO-PATTERN CONDITIONS

Investigate whether the engine can identify:

Patterned hero

AND

predominantly solid supporting pieces

AND

no competing pattern

AND

appropriate occasion/formality

AND

acceptable colour relationship.

Only where those conditions are satisfied should the pattern-safety penalty be reduced or transformed.

19. PROTECT PATTERN SAFETY

The existing pattern safety logic must continue to penalise:

stripe + check;
floral + stripe;
multiple competing patterns;
visually chaotic combinations.

Phase 3.4 demonstrated that pattern safety currently performs well in pairwise tests.

Do not damage that success.

29. IMPORTANT — DON'T GAME THE METRICS

Do not optimise solely for Top-1.

For example, a change that produces:

Top-1 53% → 60%

but causes:

Top-3 97% → 80%

is NOT automatically an improvement.

Likewise:

Top-1 +5%

with:

mean regret +3

may be a regression.

Evaluate the full metric profile.

30. QUALITY OVER SCORE MAGNITUDE

Do not attempt to make internal scores numerically resemble the external 0–100 scores.

The internal scoring system and external rubric measure different things.

Phase 3.4 established that the internal scores currently operate on a much smaller scale and that the score ceiling concern is not valid.

Do not introduce score normalisation or clamping.

31. STOP CONDITIONS

STOP and report rather than implementing further changes if:

one proposed calibration improves its target but materially damages another category;
the required change cannot be expressed without large architectural modification;
silhouette improvements require rigid body-type rules;
visual-weight detection cannot be reliably inferred from existing garment data;
pattern hierarchy requires information not currently available;
Gemini appears necessary.

Do not use Gemini as a workaround.

FINAL NON-NEGOTIABLE PRINCIPLE

Phase 3.5 is not about making AuraCloset score more.

It is about making AuraCloset make better choices.

The ideal result is not:

"The scoring formula became more sophisticated."

The ideal result is:

"When two outfits are both valid, the engine now more reliably understands why one is the better composition."

Do not add complexity unless the benchmark demonstrates measurable improvement.

Do not fix symptoms with arbitrary bonuses.

Do not sacrifice generalisation for benchmark performance.

Do not introduce Gemini.

After Phase 3.5, the next step will be:

Phase 3.6 — End-to-End Production Readiness Benchmark

That phase will test the actual production pipeline:

user → wardrobe → candidate generation → hard gates → fallback → scoring → ranking → recommendation

rather than bypassing candidate generation with predetermined outfits.

Only after Phase 3.6 will we make the final GO / NO-GO production decision.
