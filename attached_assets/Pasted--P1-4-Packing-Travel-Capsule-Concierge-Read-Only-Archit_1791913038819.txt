# P1.4 — Packing & Travel Capsule Concierge: Read-Only Architecture Audit

## Role

Act as a senior React Native/Expo architect, AI styling systems engineer, and product-minded technical lead conducting a rigorous, evidence-based architecture audit of the existing Amodka codebase.

Amodka is an existing production-oriented application, not a greenfield project. Your job is to determine the smallest credible implementation of a premium Packing & Travel Capsule Concierge using the capabilities that already exist.

**This is an audit and planning task only. Do not implement anything.**

## 1. Mandatory protections

- Make no code, configuration, documentation, database, migration, schema, security policy, dependency, or environment changes.
- Do not modify Recommendation Engine v3.7, its scoring, ranking, candidate generation, affinity, rotation, daily selection, or weather decision logic.
- Do not modify Phase A garment-group architecture, its migrations, RLS, grants, or persistence security.
- Do not add an AI provider, weather provider, database table, RPC, API route, subscription product, or package.
- Do not invoke production mutations, real purchases, or external provider operations.
- Do not run broad test suites or benchmarks simply to demonstrate activity. Inspect existing tests and use their documented results where relevant.
- Do not assume an existing feature works just because a function or UI component exists. Trace the relevant code paths and distinguish implementation evidence from unverified behaviour.
- Preserve the existing source-of-truth documentation and established project conventions.

If an unexpected issue appears, document it without fixing it.

## 2. Product objective

Assess how Amodka could provide a premium travel-planning experience that helps a woman turn her existing wardrobe into a practical, coordinated travel capsule.

The experience should potentially support:

- Destination and travel dates.
- Activities, dress codes, and occasion mix.
- Destination weather across the trip.
- Daily outfit suggestions based on wardrobe items the user actually owns.
- Reuse of garments across outfits where the existing styling system supports it.
- A packing checklist tied to the garments used in selected outfits.
- Optional travel essentials, clearly distinguished from owned wardrobe garments.
- Honest identification of wardrobe gaps without inventing items or compatibility.
- Nigerian and other African fashion, including confirmed coordinated sets and the existing Traditional styling pathway where genuinely supported.
- Existing Premium access controls.

The product ambition is an elite personal stylist and luxury fashion concierge, not a generic packing checklist. However, premium presentation must not disguise missing capabilities or unsupported styling claims.

## 3. Inspect the actual implementation

Follow the existing code and identify the relevant files, functions, types, and tests. At minimum, investigate the following areas wherever they exist:

### A. Existing Concierge and navigation
- The P1.3 occasion planner and its current transient intent model.
- `app/(tabs)/outfits.tsx`, `components/OccasionPlanner.tsx`, and `lib/concierge.ts`.
- Existing navigation, modal, loading, error, retry, and account-change patterns.
- Whether travel planning can reuse the existing experience without coupling unrelated flows.

### B. Wardrobe and African-fashion semantics
- `lib/stylingWardrobe.ts`.
- `lib/stylingRelationships.ts`.
- `lib/traditionalStyling.ts`.
- `getGarmentGroups(userId)` and the actual group-membership retrieval path.
- Canonical wardrobe fields, unknown values, user corrections, and existing ownership checks.
- How coordinated sets, multi-item garments, and independent garments are represented.
- Whether relationship-aware construction is limited to Traditional styling or also supported by generic scenarios.

Do not assume every wardrobe group is complete, every garment is correctly classified, or every multi-item relationship is usable by every styling pathway.

### C. Recommendation generation and selection
- The frozen v3.7 entry points and their actual input/output contracts.
- Existing scenario and occasion mappings.
- `AppContext` outfit pools, outfit sets, active wardrobe items, and recommendation request lifecycle.
- Daily rotation and wear-history behaviour.
- Whether the existing system can generate distinct outfit candidates for multiple trip days without mutating daily selection state, wear history, mood, saved looks, or other user data.
- Whether results can be reused or selected across multiple days without implying an optimisation algorithm that does not exist.

**Treat any inability to support a multi-day itinerary without changing frozen engine logic or corrupting daily state as an explicit architectural finding—not permission to change the engine.**

### D. Weather and location
- Existing forecast service, cache, units, location permissions, and weather-unavailable behaviour.
- The actual forecast horizon and data available per date.
- Whether destination-based, date-specific forecasts already exist.
- Timezone, date-boundary, daylight-saving, and travel-date handling.
- Celsius defaults and explicit Fahrenheit preferences.
- How the app distinguishes unavailable, stale, and genuinely available weather.

Do not assume that a current-location forecast can serve a different destination or that the provider supports every requested travel date. Do not add a provider.

### E. Premium and authentication
- Existing authoritative entitlement checks and client-side refresh behaviour.
- The P1.3 entry and submission safeguards.
- Guest, signed-in, non-Premium, expired/revoked entitlement, and unavailable-entitlement handling.
- Whether any proposed trip persistence could be correctly scoped to the authenticated owner.

Reuse the existing Premium product and entitlement architecture. Do not invent new subscription rules.

### F. Persistence and data lifecycle
- Existing saved looks and wardrobe persistence.
- Whether trip plans or packing lists already have a durable storage model.
- Account switching, sign-out, account deletion, stale results, retries, and interrupted sessions.
- The trade-offs between a transient, session-only planner and a saved/resumable trip.

Do not create a schema or persistence implementation. Recommend one approach and justify it using actual user value, engineering cost, privacy, and launch risk.

## 4. Resolve the key architecture questions

Answer each question with evidence from the codebase.

1. **Smallest credible MVP:** What is the smallest travel-concierge journey that would be genuinely useful and honest about its capabilities?

2. **Trip inputs:** Which inputs are essential for the first release? Which can be optional or deferred? Avoid collecting information that does not materially improve the result.

3. **Weather coverage:** Can existing services supply weather for the destination and each requested trip day? Define accurate behaviour for dates beyond the available forecast horizon, missing location permission, stale data, and unavailable weather.

4. **Multi-day generation:** Can existing recommendation pathways safely support several days of suggestions without changing v3.7 or mutating daily rotation, wear history, mood, or saved data? Identify the exact viable approach and limitations.

5. **Garment reuse:** Can the app reuse the same owned garment across different daily outfits? Is there an actual capsule-optimisation mechanism, or would reuse simply be an explicit, transparent selection rule? Do not claim optimisation unless code proves it.

6. **Set integrity:** How can the feature preserve confirmed coordinated-set relationships? Where does existing relationship-aware styling work, and where does it not? Do not silently break a confirmed set or claim generic relationship awareness that the code does not provide.

7. **Packing checklist:** Can a checklist be derived reliably from selected outfit garments? Explain how to prevent duplicate garment entries and how to distinguish:
   - owned garments selected for outfits;
   - optional non-wardrobe travel essentials;
   - genuine wardrobe gaps or suggested purchases.

   Do not invent wardrobe items or imply the user owns a suggested item.

8. **Persistence:** Should the first release be transient or support saving and resuming trips? Make one recommendation and state what evidence would justify revisiting it.

9. **Premium boundaries:** Where must entitlement be checked, and how should access loss or entitlement-service failure be handled?

10. **African-fashion integrity:** How will the flow preserve canonical user-confirmed data and existing Traditional styling behaviour without making assumptions about culture, ethnicity, dress obligations, or mandatory garments?

11. **Failure and empty states:** Specify behaviour for insufficient wardrobe data, no viable outfit, missing forecast, failed garment-group retrieval, interrupted generation, stale requests, and account changes.

12. **Privacy and scope:** What destination, travel-date, location, or trip information would be processed or stored? What is the least data necessary for the proposed MVP?

## 5. Separate MVP from future enhancements

Produce two clearly separated lists.

### P1.4 MVP candidates
Only capabilities supported by the current architecture, or that can be added through a narrowly bounded change that leaves frozen systems untouched.

### Later enhancements
Potentially include persistent trip plans, richer multi-day capsule optimisation, destination-specific forecast integrations, shopping recommendations, conversational AI, or more advanced packing intelligence—but only where justified.

For each MVP candidate, classify it as:

- **Already supported:** verified existing code path.
- **Bounded addition:** feasible without changing protected systems; requires implementation.
- **Blocked:** requires an architectural capability or decision not currently available.

Do not label a capability “already supported” solely because a similar component exists.

## 6. Required output

Return a concise but technically substantive report with these sections:

1. **Executive verdict:** Proceed, proceed with constraints, or stop pending a blocker.
2. **Evidence-based current-state map:** Relevant files, functions, responsibilities, and actual limitations.
3. **Proposed minimum user journey:** From opening the Concierge to obtaining outfits and a packing checklist.
4. **Architecture feasibility matrix:** For each critical capability, include status, evidence, limitations, and recommended treatment.
5. **Frozen-engine safety assessment:** Explain exactly how multi-day planning can or cannot operate without changing v3.7 or contaminating daily state.
6. **Persistence decision:** Transient versus durable trip plans, with justification.
7. **Failure-state and privacy requirements.**
8. **MVP versus later enhancements.**
9. **File-level implementation plan:** Proposed files to reuse, files that may need new code, and the responsibility of each change. Do not create these files.
10. **Acceptance criteria:** Observable, testable conditions that a later implementation must meet.
11. **Risks and unresolved questions:** Prioritised by severity and backed by evidence.
12. **Recommended next step:** The smallest sensible implementation milestone, or the exact blocker that must be resolved first.

For important findings, cite repository file paths and specific function/type names. State when evidence is unavailable. Clearly label inference and recommendations separately from verified implementation facts.

## 7. Stop condition and final confirmation

Stop when the evidence is sufficient to decide feasibility, scope, architecture boundaries, and the next milestone.

Do not implement the feature, make “small fixes,” or continue into P1.5. Do not report tests as passing unless they were actually run during this audit.

End with a short confirmation stating:

- No files or configuration were changed.
- No migrations or database operations were performed.
- No providers or dependencies were added.
- Recommendation Engine v3.7 and Phase A were left untouched.
- Which important questions remain unverified.
